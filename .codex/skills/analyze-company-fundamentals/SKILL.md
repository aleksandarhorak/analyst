# Analyze Company Fundamentals

## Inputs

Establish the company and security identifiers, reporting currency, fiscal
calendar, analysis horizon, as-of date, peer set, and available filings. Use
`verify-financial-evidence` when sources or timing are not already controlled.

## Procedure

1. Map products, customers, geographies, suppliers, regulation, industry
   structure, competitive advantages, disruption risks, and cyclicality.
2. Build a driver tree from operational units and prices to revenue, margins,
   reinvestment, free cash flow, and returns on incremental capital.
3. Reconcile income statement, balance sheet, cash flow, segments, and footnotes
   across enough periods to see cycles and accounting changes.
4. Test earnings quality: accruals, working capital, cash conversion,
   capitalization, recurring adjustments, reserves, related parties, auditor or
   control issues, and management incentives.
5. Evaluate leverage, covenants, maturities, liquidity, off-balance-sheet
   obligations, dilution, pension or contingent claims, and distress paths.
6. Assess capital allocation, governance, ownership, compensation, transactions
   with insiders, and research or banking conflicts.
7. Compare with economically relevant peers while explaining accounting,
   business-mix, and cycle differences.
8. Identify the strongest evidence against the emerging view and define what
   must be monitored.

Use [the company-analysis template](references/company-analysis.md). Hand
forecast and valuation work to `value-company-and-forecast`.

## Rules

- Do not turn a historical factor score into an automatic recommendation.
- Distinguish reported, adjusted, and analyst-normalized values.
- Show material reconciliations; never accept management's non-standard metric
  without tracing it to the statements.
- State when the history is too short, structurally changed, or incomparable.

## Output

Conclude with business quality, financial health, accounting confidence,
management and governance assessment, key dependencies, red flags,
disconfirmers, and the fundamental drivers required for valuation.
