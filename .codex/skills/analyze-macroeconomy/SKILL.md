# Analyze Macroeconomy

## Procedure

1. Define economies, variables, currencies, horizon, market cutoff, and whether
   the task is description, forecasting, scenario analysis, or causal inference.
2. Build a release-vintage ledger. Separate event time, publication time,
   consensus available before release, first release, and revision.
3. Normalize units, seasonal adjustment, annualization, frequency, base
   effects, nominal versus real values, and levels versus rates of change.
4. Diagnose the regime across growth, inflation, labor, monetary and fiscal
   policy, credit, financial conditions, external balances, and exchange rates.
5. Map transmission through cash flows, discount rates, funding, currency,
   commodities, and risk appetite; state lags and competing mechanisms.
6. Compare forecasts with simple benchmarks and evaluate genuinely
   out-of-sample at the relevant horizon. Use density or scenario forecasts.
7. Grade causal claims by identification strength; do not infer policy effects
   from timing or correlation alone.
8. Report regime-break risk, disconfirmers, scenario triggers, and what data
   could change the view.

Use [the macro-analysis template](references/macro-analysis.md).

## Rules

- Never backfill a historical call with a later revised series.
- Treat yield-curve, survey, market, or composite indicators as conditional
  evidence, not deterministic rules.
- Explain country, sample, structural, and policy-regime limits before
  generalizing a historical relationship.

## Output

Provide the as-of snapshot, vintages, regime assessment, baseline and
alternative scenarios, forecast distribution, transmission map, asset or
company implications, confidence, disconfirmers, and next release triggers.
