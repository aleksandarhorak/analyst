# Market Behavior Research

## Problem Summary

This research defines how the financial agent may use behavioral-finance
evidence when interpreting current prices, news, volume, volatility, positioning,
and likely market reactions. The objective is not to diagnose investors or turn
popular labels such as fear, greed, or FOMO into forecasts. It is to connect a
specific, observable mechanism to evidence, state the relevant trader group and
horizon, and preserve uncertainty.

## Scope

- Review primary research on reference dependence, attention, sentiment,
  information diffusion, overconfidence, disposition, extrapolation, media
  effects, excess volatility, and herding.
- Translate supported mechanisms into a bounded market-behavior analysis.
- Identify counterevidence and external-validity limits that prevent automatic
  directional calls.
- Define the evidence and abstention controls required for symbol research.

## Non-Goals

- Predicting prices from a psychological story alone.
- Treating all retail, institutional, systematic, or short-term traders alike.
- Inferring private mental states from a price move.
- Treating an historical anomaly as current, tradable alpha.
- Replacing fundamental, macroeconomic, news, liquidity, or risk analysis.

## Research Status

Complete as of 2026-07-31. Eleven primary papers were reviewed in full. The
review deliberately includes evidence against broad institutional-herding
claims. Findings are recorded as conditional mechanisms with explicit limits,
not as universal laws.
